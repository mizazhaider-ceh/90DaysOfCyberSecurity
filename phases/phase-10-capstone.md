# Phase 10: Capstone: Prove Your Skills

This is it. The last six days of the plan are where you prove everything you learned by doing real offensive work. You will run full attack chains end to end in the PenTrix VulnLab, take on the hardest modules, publish a portfolio project, and close out the 90 days with a clear picture of where you stand and where you go next.

## Day 85: Kill Chain One, End to End
- **Learn:** A kill chain is the full path of an attack, from first contact with a target to reaching the final objective. Running one cleanly means you plan your steps, stay organized, and can explain what you did.
- **Do:** Start the PenTrix VulnLab from https://github.com/mizazhaider-ceh/PenTrix-VulnLab, pick one of the three kill-chain scenarios, and complete it from start to finish. Write down every step in a notebook file as you go: what you scanned, what you found, which exploit worked, and what flag you earned.
- [ ] Done: one full kill-chain scenario completed, with every step documented and the flag earned.

## Day 86: Kill Chain Two, Compare the Paths
- **Learn:** Different attack paths can reach the same goal, but they differ in speed, stealth, and tools needed. Comparing paths builds the judgment that separates a beginner from a professional tester.
- **Do:** Complete a second kill-chain scenario in the PenTrix VulnLab from https://github.com/mizazhaider-ceh/PenTrix-VulnLab. Then write a short comparison of the two scenarios: which step was hardest in each, which tools you reused, and what you would do differently next time.
- [ ] Done: second scenario finished, plus a written comparison of both attack paths.

## Day 87: Beat the Modules You Skipped
- **Learn:** OAuth flows, crypto weaknesses, and deserialization bugs are some of the most valuable findings in real web testing. They look scary, but each one breaks down into a few repeatable patterns.
- **Do:** Open the PenTrix VulnLab from https://github.com/mizazhaider-ceh/PenTrix-VulnLab and pick the OAuth, crypto, and deserialization modules you avoided earlier. Work through them patiently and earn flags in at least three of these modules.
- [ ] Done: flags earned in at least 3 of the hard modules (OAuth, crypto, deserialization), with notes on how each exploit worked.

## Day 88: A Professional Challenge Writeup
- **Learn:** A good writeup tells a story: what you found, how you found it, how you exploited it, and how the owner should fix it. Employers and the community judge your skill by your writeups.
- **Do:** Pick one web challenge on https://tryhackme.com or https://www.hackthebox.com and solve it. Then write a full professional writeup with four sections: recon, exploitation, proof of impact, and remediation advice written as if the owner of the system will read it.
- [ ] Done: one solved challenge with a complete four-section writeup (recon, exploitation, proof of impact, remediation).

## Day 89: Ship a Portfolio Project on GitHub
- **Learn:** Projects are proof. A small, clean tool or a well-written set of lab writeups on your GitHub profile says more about you than any certificate list.
- **Do:** Build and publish one mini project on your GitHub account: a small Python scanner (for example, one that checks a list of URLs for common security headers), a Burp Suite cheat sheet in markdown, or your best lab writeups organized as a portfolio repo. Include a README that explains what it does and how to run it.
- [ ] Done: one public GitHub repo published with working code or writeups and a clear README.

## Day 90: Retrospective, Resume, and Next 90 Days
- **Learn:** Skills only stick when you review them. A retrospective turns 90 days of scattered wins into a clear story you can tell in interviews, and fresh goals keep you moving.
- **Do:** Write a one-page retrospective: the three skills that improved most, the hardest thing you solved, and one weakness to fix next. Then update your resume with real skills and your new projects (PenTrix scenarios, the writeup, the GitHub repo). Finish by writing three concrete goals for the next 90 days.
- [ ] Done: one-page retrospective written, resume updated with real skills and projects, and three goals set for the next 90 days.
