# Build Your Resume

Days 91 and 92 turn everything you learned in the first 90 days into a one-page resume that a hiring manager can actually read. You built real skills (Linux, networking, web attacks, Python, SIEM), so now you present them like real experience. A junior SOC analyst resume is not about years of work, it is about proof.

## Day 91: Draft your one-page cybersecurity resume
- **Learn:** A good resume has seven parts in this order: header (name, location, phone, email, GitHub and LinkedIn links), summary (2 to 3 lines about who you are and what you can do), skills (grouped: operating systems, networking, security tools, programming), projects (your strongest proof of skill), experience (internships, part-time jobs, or home lab work written like job duties), education (school, expected graduation date), and certifications (earned or in progress, with the expected month). Hiring managers skim for about 10 seconds, so every line must earn its place.
- **Do:** Open a free Google Docs resume template and fill in all seven sections, keeping the whole resume to one page. In the projects section, add three entries from your 90 days: PenTrix VulnLab (number of flags earned, types of vulnerabilities exploited like SQL injection and XSS), a Python tool you built (what it does, for example "scans a subnet and reports open ports"), and your SIEM home lab (which log sources you collected, how many alerts you wrote, which attack you detected). Start every bullet with an action verb (built, detected, analyzed, automated, investigated) and add a number wherever you can (flags, hosts, alerts, lines of code). Leave out: your photo, your full address, your date of birth, high school details, hobbies, and any skill you cannot demo if asked in an interview.

- [ ] Done: I have a one-page draft in Google Docs with all seven sections filled in, and the projects section lists the VulnLab flags, the Python tool, and the SIEM lab with numbers and action verbs.

## Day 92: Polish and get your profiles ready
- **Learn:** Tailoring means mirroring the job post: if a junior SOC analyst posting asks for "SIEM triage, log analysis, and ticket documentation", those exact words should appear in your summary and skills section. Your GitHub and LinkedIn are your second and third pages, so a clean GitHub (pinned repos, real READMEs, no empty or broken projects) and a finished LinkedIn (headline, about section, skills, profile photo) matter as much as the resume.
- **Do:** Pick one real junior SOC analyst job posting and copy 5 to 8 of its keywords (for example: SIEM, log analysis, incident triage, Splunk, phishing analysis) into your resume where they honestly fit. Then clean up GitHub: pin your 3 best repos, make sure each has a README with a description, setup steps, and a screenshot. Then finish LinkedIn: write a headline like "Cybersecurity student | SOC analyst track | SIEM, Linux, Python" and a short About section (3 to 4 sentences on what you built during your 90-day plan). Finally, send the resume to one friend or a free cybersecurity community (like a Discord server or a Reddit resume review thread) and apply at least 3 pieces of their feedback the same day.
